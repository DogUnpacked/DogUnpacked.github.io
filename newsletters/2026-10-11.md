You pick up the leash. Your German Shepherd heads for the door. Then you stop to answer a message.

The whine that follows sounds a lot like an opinion.

His breed's working history is worth knowing. German Shepherds developed from sheep-tending dogs in Germany. They patrolled the edges of unfenced grazing land, keeping flocks of hundreds of sheep out of neighboring crops: a living fence, working all day alongside a shepherd. The job called for stamina, independent decisions, and close cooperation with a human partner. [More on the tending history](https://www.gsdcoregon.com/breedinfo.html)

That history tells you what he was built for. It doesn't decode every sound he makes. At home, whining can come with excitement, a request for attention, anxiety, or pain, [according to the ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/whining). The clues are in what happens around it: the leash coming out, a door closing, your attention moving elsewhere.

And that whine can sound remarkably sad. In a 2019 study in Royal Society Open Science, 561 young adults rated recordings of dog whines about as sad as recordings of babies crying. That tells you how people hear the sound, not what prompted it. [Read the study](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.181555)

Tonight, the next time he whines, notice what happened just before, what his body is doing, and what happens next. Repeated situations give you clues about what prompts the sound. If the whining is new, persistent, or comes with signs of discomfort, check with your vet.
