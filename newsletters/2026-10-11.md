You tell him to get off the couch and he answers. A groan, a grumble, a long whine that sounds a lot like an opinion. Your German Shepherd isn't being rude. He's bred that way.

German Shepherds come from tending work in Germany: flocks of hundreds of sheep grazing on unfenced ground right beside crops. The dog patrolled the edge of the grazing like a living fence while the shepherd led the flock and talked to him in a soft, conversational voice. It was a working partnership with one person, all day, about every change on the field.

At home, that person is you. The grumble when the walk ends early, the whine at the door at the same time every evening, the groan when you sit down instead of moving: often that's his side of the conversation about a change in the routine he's tracking.

You're not the only one who finds the whine hard to ignore. In a 2019 study in Royal Society Open Science, 561 young adults rated recorded animal sounds, and dog whines were rated as sad as a human baby's cry. [Read the study](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.181555)

Tonight, note the next five times he whines or grumbles: where, what time, what just changed. A pattern tells you what he's tracking. If the whining is new or won't settle, check with a vet.
