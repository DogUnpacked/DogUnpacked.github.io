Wire Fox Terrier

The job

The Wire Fox Terrier was developed to bolt the fox from its lair so the hunt could continue.

Source: American Kennel Club

Facts

  1. Fox Terriers took shape in the late 1700s, during the heyday of British foxhunts, and their job was to bolt the fox from its lair.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  2. The coat is mostly white, and red is not allowed, so the dog is not mistaken for a fox during a hunt.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  3. Paintings show that both the wire and smooth coats were around by the middle of the 18th century.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  4. The AKC breed standard lists height as 15½ inches and weight as 18 pounds (male), 15-17 (female).

    Source: American Kennel Club

  5. The American Kennel Club lists 1885 as the year this breed was recognized.

    Source: American Kennel Club

What that means at home

In a house, that earth-dog job can look like investigating dens and tight spaces and staying on the task.
