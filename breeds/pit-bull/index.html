American Pit Bull Terrier

The job

The American Pit Bull Terrier comes from nineteenth-century crosses of bulldogs and terriers in England, Ireland, and Scotland. In the United States, farmers and ranchers used these dogs to catch semi-wild cattle and hogs, to hunt, and to drive livestock.

Source: United Kennel Club

Facts

  1. UKC history says fanciers in England, Ireland, and Scotland began crossing bulldogs and terriers in the nineteenth century.

    Source: United Kennel Club

  2. Immigrants brought those bull-and-terrier crosses to the United States, where farmers and ranchers used them as catch dogs for semi-wild cattle and hogs, to hunt, and to drive livestock.

    Source: United Kennel Club

  3. The United Kennel Club was the first registry to recognize the American Pit Bull Terrier. Founder C. Z. Bennett assigned registration number 1 to his own dog, Bennett's Ring, in 1898.

    Source: United Kennel Club

  4. The UKC standard describes a medium-sized, short-coated dog whose body is just slightly longer than tall, built as a catch dog that can hold, push, pull, and breathe while working.

    Source: United Kennel Club

  5. The UKC standard gives a desirable weight of 35 to 60 pounds for a mature male and 30 to 50 pounds for a mature female, and a height guide of 18 to 21 inches for males and 17 to 20 inches for females.

    Source: United Kennel Club

What that means at home

At home, a dog bred to catch and drive livestock still wants a physical job. A walk with work in it, and a clear start and finish to that work, fits the original use.
