Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen

The job

The Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen was developed as a hound for dense, thorny, rocky country, at a pace hunters on foot could follow.

Source: American Kennel Club

Facts

  1. Griffon Vendéen hounds developed over more than 400 years into the coated French hounds known today.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  2. The dense, thorny, rocky region of the Vendeé called for a hardy dog whose coat could resist brambles.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  3. Hunters who had no horses needed a slower hound so they could keep up on foot.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  4. The AKC breed standard lists height as 15.5-18 inches and weight as 40-45 pounds.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  5. The American Kennel Club lists 2018 as the year this breed was recognized.

    Source: American Kennel Club

What that means at home

In a house, that hunting work can look like a dog who wants to follow a scent through rough cover and keep a pace a person can share. A long walk on foot, with time to trail, gives the old hound job a place to go.
