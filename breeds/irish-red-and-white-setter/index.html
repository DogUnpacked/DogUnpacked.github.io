Irish Red and White Setter

The job

The Irish Red and White Setter was developed to creep close to game birds and hold a set so a hunter could net them.

Source: American Kennel Club

Facts

  1. Before the all-red Irish Setter, the red and white dog was a fixture of Ireland's hills and bogs since at least the 1600s.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  2. Setters crawled toward game birds, froze in a setting position, and signaled with the tail until a hunter threw a net.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  3. When nets gave way to firearms, setter lines adapted that crouching style of hunting.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  4. The AKC breed standard lists height as 24.5-26 inches (male), 22.5-24 inches (female) and weight as 42-60 pounds (male), 35-50 pounds (female).

    Source: American Kennel Club

  5. The American Kennel Club lists 2009 as the year this breed was recognized.

    Source: American Kennel Club

What that means at home

In a house, that setting work can look like a dog who goes still when a scent is worth reporting, and who likes a long, careful search.
