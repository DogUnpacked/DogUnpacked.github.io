English Cocker Spaniel

The job

The English Cocker Spaniel was bred as a smaller spaniel to hunt woodcock.

Source: American Kennel Club

Facts

  1. In the late 1800s, spaniels were split into springers and cockers, and the cockers were the smaller dogs bred to hunt woodcock.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  2. Into the early 20th century, English Cockers and Springer Spaniels were told apart by height alone.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  3. The American Kennel Club recognized the English Cocker Spaniel as a separate breed in 1946.

    Source: American Kennel Club

  4. The AKC breed standard lists height as 16-17 inches (male), 15-16 inches (female) and weight as 28-34 pounds (male), 26-32 pounds (female).

    Source: American Kennel Club

What that means at home

At home, that woodcock work can look like a dog who wants to hunt through brush and stay close enough for a person to follow. A walk in grass and cover, with a retrieve at the end, is the home version of the old bird job.
